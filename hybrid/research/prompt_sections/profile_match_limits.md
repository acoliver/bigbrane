P3 LIMITS. A P3 statement carries `profile_slot.selected_limits`, the limits record the statement fixer froze, and `instantiated_claim`, the slot's claim with that limit filled in. Compare the formal statement with `instantiated_claim`, read together with the definitions it uses.

The slot template is existential: "at least one task family the mechanism cannot solve". A statement instantiates it by naming one task family (in the limit and its definitions) and proving that no setting of the mechanism within the stated resources solves every member of it. Its formal statement is then universal over the mechanism's settings, readouts or weights, and often over the sizes, and concrete in the task. That shape is the instantiation itself, not a quantifier difference: "for every readout, the instance at size V is answered wrong" proves "this task family cannot be solved" for the chosen family. Mark quantifiers "differs" only when the formal statement quantifies the task, the sizes or the resources differently from `instantiated_claim` (for example: it proves the failure for some readout only, or at a size the limit does not state, or under a resource the limit does not name).

Judge the statement against the criteria the template and the limit state, and no others. Whether the limit is interesting, deep or the one known for this model is not part of the comparison; do not mark a statement "weaker" or "different" for being simple or for not being the limit you expected.

One profile rule applies here (FIX-17, decision P3): the limit must be about the mechanism, its state, update or readout, as the limit's `basis` says. Mark `hypotheses` "differs" when the formal statement's failure follows only from the output vocabulary (for example: a single output token cannot name a number larger than the vocabulary) or from how the task's tokens are encoded, and not from the mechanism part the basis names; quote the statement text that shows what the failure rests on, and the basis quote, in the objection.
