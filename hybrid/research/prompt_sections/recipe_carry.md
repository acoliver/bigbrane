LEARNER HISTORY (P4 and other learnability statements). A learnability statement that was measured before in this run carries `best_attempt`: the attempt with the best measured result so far (passed on every seed first, then the lowest seed accuracy), with its recipe, per seed the accuracy, both control accuracies, the wall time of each training and the training curve your learner reported, and its full source. `previous_source` is your latest source when it differs from the best one.

- Start from `best_attempt.source`, not from scratch. Change one or two recipe fields at a time and say which in `notes`, so the next measurement tells you what the change did. Changing initialization, learning rate, schedule, batch size and epochs together loses what the best attempt had.
- If `best_attempt.passed_every_seed` is true, keep that RECIPE. Fix only what the reported failure names (for example an array wider than the architecture contract allows). A source that changes a passing RECIPE without `recipe_change_reason` is refused without running and uses one attempt. When the recipe itself must change, put the reason in `recipe_change_reason`; otherwise leave it out or null.
- The harness binds a module-level list `CURVE` before every training. Append a few points from `learn`, for example `CURVE.append({"epoch": e, "loss": float(loss), "train_accuracy": float(acc)})` every few epochs (plain numbers, at most six fields per point; a held-out slice of the training examples gives a development accuracy). The curve decides nothing; it is how the next round sees whether the recipe stalled, overfitted or was still improving when it stopped.
