MECHANISM DEFINITIONS. A definition that restates the card's own mechanism (its carried state, its update, its readout or its learning rule, with the sizes the card gives them) has kind "mechanism". It fixes the object the claims are about, so it is part of the claims, not a hidden premise, and the statement-match checker reads it against the card's mechanism text.

- Keep a mechanism definition faithful to the card: the card's state, equations and learning rule, in the card's own sizes. Do not add state, buffers, steps or abilities the card does not have, and do not state a result (an accuracy, a capacity, a bound, a cost) in it: a result is a statement to check or a premise.
- Put choices the card leaves open (how ties break, which position is scored, how an underspecified width is chosen) in a separate definition of kind evaluation, accounting or notation, so each definition is either the card's mechanism or a convention, never a mix.
- Give the numerical semantics their own definition (FIX-13): the precision, and which finite-precision evaluations of the equations a witness may use. Unless the card restricts the evaluation order, exact algebraic identities are allowed and the definition says so, for example subtracting a common shift from all softmax scores or normalizing through log-sum-exp. If the card does restrict it, the definition states the restriction, and a witness that overflows under it fails. This definition is frozen before any witness is written, so the prover and the challenger read the same rule.
- An operation sequence chosen only to make P1's exact count simple (a product spelling, a softmax without max subtraction) is not part of the mechanism. It goes in a definition with `"scope": "cost_reference"`, cited only by cost and conformance statements.
