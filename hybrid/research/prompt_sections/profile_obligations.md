THE TRANSFORMER PROFILE (Phase 4). Besides the card's own claims, this card must discharge eleven profile obligations, listed among the claims to formalize with the ids P1, P2.copy, P2.recall, P2.nesting, P2.composition, P3, P4.copy, P4.recall, P4.nesting, P4.composition and P5. They hold the card to what is known about the model it must beat. Their shape is fixed: you fill in the card's numbers and formulas, never the forms, sizes or thresholds. A statement of a form the slot does not accept, or one that leaves out a required size, is dropped before freezing, and a slot left without statements leaves the card unproven. For a profile obligation, claim_quote quotes the slot's text from the template below (not the card).

How to write each slot, using the card's mechanism exactly as the card defines it:

- P1: one cost statement. `ops` is the total counted operations of the card's update and readout over n tokens, `state` the peak inference state words, both in n and the card's own size parameters, `sizes` the card's cost size names (resources.cost.sizes) on the protocol grid (PROTOCOL VALUES), `exponent` the exponent in n of `ops`. Leave `ns` out so the verifier runs the plan's lengths 64 to 4096. Derive the formula from the card's equations in the units of the P1 COUNTING CONVENTION below, which is what the checker counts: decide whether each product is written with `@` (k units per output element) or as multiply then sum (2k-1), say which in an accounting definition with `"scope": "cost_reference"` (together with any other choice made only so the count is exact, such as a softmax without max subtraction), cite that definition only from P1 and its conformance statement, and count every Python number the state keeps (a position counter is one word). The cost-reference recipe never binds a P2 or P4 witness: those cite the mechanism and numerical-contract definitions. If the reference subtracts a maximum, the max reduction and the subtraction are counted like any other operation. `init` never receives n, so the state formula is the peak of what the state holds as tokens arrive, not a reservation sized by n. An activation the accounting charges one operation must be one the counter charges one unit: `ops.np.tanh`, and `ops.np.sigmoid` for the logistic sigma(x) = 1/(1+exp(-x)); a sigmoid written out from exp costs its expanded units. The prover's reference code is counted against the formula and the checks are exact: a formula that is off by any term fails. The one allowance is a declared remainder (`ops_remainder` or `state_remainder`) for lower-order work you cannot state exactly, with `ops_remainder_lower_in` or `state_remainder_lower_in` naming the sizes in which it is of strictly lower order than the formula; the host refuses before freezing a remainder that is negative, not below its formula on the grid, or not of lower order in a named size. A remainder never covers a leading-term difference such as the `@` versus multiply-then-sum choice.
- P2.copy, P2.recall, P2.nesting: one construction statement each (the witness follows the mechanism's equations under the numerical contract, not the P1 cost-reference recipe) (tasks copy, recall, dyck), with every required size listed and `state_words`, the construction's peak state words as a formula in the task's size (L, K or depth), copied from the card's resources.tasks entry with its d and rounds (FIX-16: CARD RESOURCES). P2.composition: two construction statements, one for task composition (size T) and one for task binding (size hops). State the resources the card's mechanism needs, from the TASK CONTRACTS below: the token stream length at every size (composition and binding count both tables, the separators and the queries), the layout's vocabulary, the key universe against the pair count. A mechanism that keeps rows per input token declares `token_store`, checked against the real streams before freezing. The width, rounds and state words are the card's (CARD RESOURCES): copy them, never choose them. When the card says the mechanism cannot reach a size within its fixed resources, still list the size: the construction is then expected to fail there, and that failure is the result.
- P3: one to three derivation statements (lean, sympy or z3) of a limit, plus the `limits` list: for each limit, the task (a construction task, or "other" for a task family outside them, such as parity), the smallest size at which the mechanism cannot solve it within its stated resources (null for "other"), the resources it would need, and why. A limit on a construction task must not be contradicted by P2: a construction that meets the threshold at or above the stated size fails P3. Every finite mechanism has some limit; claiming none fails P3. Each limit also states its `basis` (FIX-17, decision P3): the part of the mechanism it follows from (`part`: "state", "update" or "readout"), the `anchor` of that part in the card (mechanism.state.<name>, mechanism.update.<name> or mechanism.readout.<name>) and a `quote` of the card's text there. A limit must be about the mechanism: what its state can hold, what its update can compute, or what its readout can report from its state. A limit that follows only from the output vocabulary (for example a single-token readout over V symbols that cannot report the number V) or from the task encoding (how the tokens of a task are laid out) is not a limit of the mechanism; give it `part` "output_vocabulary" or "task_encoding" if that is all it rests on, and it is refused at freeze. Replace it with a limit of the mechanism itself.
- P4.copy, P4.recall, P4.nesting, P4.composition: one learnability statement each, at the solvable case (copy L <= 4, recall K <= 4, depth 2, composition T <= 4 or binding hops <= 2), with `size` and `examples` (the number of training examples m the card claims its own learning rule needs) copied from the card's resources.learning entry, and the protocol seeds. State m from the card; it is fixed before anything runs. When the card states no m for a task, put that slot in not_formalized with that reason rather than inventing a count. Freeze the model class in a definition (the card's architecture, its learning rule and the widths the card states) and leave the training recipe to the prover: initialization, optimizer, learning rate, epochs or passes and batch size are chosen by the prover within the P4 budget stated below, declared in the learner's RECIPE, and applied identically to the true data and both controls (see P4 TRAINING RECIPE). Do not freeze an optimizer schedule the card does not state. Never change m, the seeds, the threshold or the controls to fit the budget.
- P5: one conservation statement with the card's numbers: each entry's `state_bits` derived by the host from the card's resources (leave it out; FIX-16), a copy entry {"D", "L", "accuracy"} at every P2 copy length, a recall entry {"K", "V", "accuracy"} at every P2 recall size, a dyck entry {"depth", "accuracy"} at every P2 depth (and at any size a P2 statement adds), each with its accuracy at that size and the resources it has there, copy entries with the D and recall entries with the V of the P2 construction. An entry's accuracy is either "ceiling" or a number with "claim" (FIX-11). Write `"accuracy": "ceiling"` when the card states no accuracy at that size, or says only that the task fails or is exact there: the host fills in, before freezing, the largest accuracy the conservation law allows the entry's state_bits at that size. Write a number only when a card claim states that accuracy at that size, and name it in `"claim"` with the claim id from CLAIMS TO FORMALIZE (a profile slot is not a card claim): for a card that claims chance accuracy 1/16 for recall at K = 64, {"K": 64, "V": 16, "accuracy": 0.0625, "state_bits": 4, "claim": "capability.4"}. The host refuses a number with no claim id or with an id that is not a card claim. Never write the P2 threshold 0.99 where the card claims failure: P5 records what the card claims, and P2 measures what a construction does. An entry at a larger size does not account for a smaller one: a card whose state holds 4 bits writes dyck entries at depths 2, 4, 8 and 16 (with "ceiling" the host fills in 1, 1, 0.0625 and 0.000244140625), not only the depth-16 entry. The host refuses a P5 statement with a missing size before freezing. Add a sample entry {"log2_H", "prior_bits", "bits_per_example", "examples"} for every P4 example count, with structure built into the mechanism counted as prior bits. If a P2 construction meets the threshold at a size P5 does not cover with at least that accuracy, P5 fails.

The P2 sizes are the same for every card and are chosen so that small fixed-state mechanisms fail some of them. Write the statements for what the card's mechanism can and cannot do; do not shrink the sizes, lower the thresholds, or describe a different mechanism.
