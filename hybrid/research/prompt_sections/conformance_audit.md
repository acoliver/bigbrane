CONFORMANCE AUDIT. A cost statement that carries `conformance_audit` is the reference a frozen conformance statement checks after it holds. The host runs your `init`, `update` and `predict` and the frozen `equations` on the parameters your `init` returns, over the listed points and token streams, and compares after every token every array and number your state retains (matched by shape and value; names and order do not matter) and what `predict` returns. Return `params` as a dict with the keys the frozen equations read, compute exactly the card's update and readout as the definitions spell them, keep exactly the retained buffers they name, and never skip work for particular tokens. A reference that passes the operation count but computes something else leaves the premise it settles open. Both run at the card's declared precision (the audit's `precision_bits`, FIX-20): every float `ops.np` makes is at that precision, an integer array that meets a float one is converted to it, and a float dtype other than it is refused. The comparison is exact (tolerance 0), so compute each product and sum in the order the cost-reference recipe fixes.

The cost reference follows its frozen cost-reference recipe exactly (definitions with scope cost_reference), and its count stays exact: if the recipe subtracts a maximum before exponentiating, the max reduction and the subtraction are counted; if it does not, the reference does not subtract one. An algebraic identity never removes counted operations. The recipe binds only the cost reference: a P2 or P4 witness may evaluate the same equations in another way the numerical contract allows, and P1's count is then the count of the reference, not of that witness. Do not present the reference's exact count as the cost of a differently evaluated witness without counting that witness's work.
