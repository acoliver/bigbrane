PROFILE SLOTS (Phase 4). Some claims above are transformer-profile slots (claim ids P1 to P5) rather than the card's own words; their items carry `profile_slot`. A slot's text is a fixed template. Its forms, tasks, required sizes, thresholds, seeds and solvable cases are fixed. Its formulas, declared state words, limits, example counts and conservation numbers are parameters the statement fixer filled in from the card. Compare a slot statement as follows.

- A concrete value that fills a parameter the template leaves open is not a difference in constants or exponents. An exact operation-count polynomial in n and the card's sizes is the symbolic formula P1 asks for; one specific limit with its size and derivation is the "at least one task family" P3 asks for. Check instead that the filled-in values say what the card says.
- A slot that names several tasks (P2.composition and P4.composition: two-hop lookup and chained assignments) is covered by several statements, one per task, and holds only when every one of them holds. `profile_slot.part` names the task this statement covers and `profile_slot.statements` lists the slot's statements. Compare the statement with its own part of the slot text, not with the whole slot.
- A learnability statement fixes the model class, m, the seeds, the threshold and the controls; the training recipe (initialization, optimizer, learning rate, epochs, batch size) is chosen by the prover and is not part of the statement, so its absence is not a difference.
- Everything the template fixes must match exactly: the task, every required size, the threshold, the number of seeds and the solvable case. A missing required size, a lower threshold, another task or a different form is a mismatch.
- P5 copy, recall and dyck entries (FIX-11): an entry with `"ceiling": true` holds the host-computed information ceiling of its state_bits at that size, not a number from the card, so compare only its sizes, D, V and state_bits with the card. An entry with `"claim"` cites the card claim that states its accuracy at that size: check that the cited claim states that accuracy at that size (a claim that the task fails there does not state 0.99), and report a cited claim that does not support the number as a difference in constants.
