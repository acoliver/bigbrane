# Stronger models can justify thinner scaffolding; verification still needs an independent boundary

The recent evidence supports testing a capable coding model with less prescribed search, planning and multi-agent orchestration, especially for ML engineering and computational discovery. The closest matches to the requested papers are **AgentDiscover** (October 4), **Harness Annealing** (October 1), and **How Much of a Harness Does a Strong Agent Need for Autonomous ML Engineering?** (September 30). Their experiments support specific reductions in capability scaffolding, with tools and evaluation infrastructure retained. They do not establish a general law that larger models make harnesses irrelevant or justify removing execution safeguards and independent scoring. Counter-evidence includes harness gains for stronger models on open-ended repository tasks, improvements from task-adaptive harnesses, and verification-dependent gains in harness optimization. For Bigbrane, keep search policy open to simplification while retaining the exact-oracle evaluator, protected confirmation lane, schemas and attempt ledger.

## Scope, dates and terminology

Read-only `date` returned `Fri Oct 9 17:02:35 -03 2026`. The search window was **August 1 through October 9, 2026**, emphasizing October. Eight papers are included, four from October, three from September, and one first submitted in August with a September revision. All linked arXiv texts were opened. Methods, results and relevant appendices were read from the papers themselves, using section-level retrieval where the web tool truncated the full HTML. Search summaries helped locate sources; the findings below rely on the primary texts. These are author-reported results, without independent reproduction. No papers from 2024 or 2025 are used as substitutes.

Three meanings of “controls” need separation. **Capability scaffolding** includes prescribed planners, parent-selection policies, subagents, retry routines and context management. **Development verification** supplies feedback that helps improve a draft, candidate or harness edit. **Trust boundaries** determine who can execute code, read hidden data, modify scoring, or authorize a final scientific claim. A reduction in the first category does not establish that the other two can be removed. AgentDiscover directly documents failures in evaluator boundaries.

“More capable” also needs care. Most studies compare model generations, training recipes or different backbones, with size, post-training and tool fluency changing together. Harness Annealing explicitly studies two model scales, but its 35B model is a mixture-of-experts model with 3B active parameters. None of these results isolates parameter count as a universal cause of declining harness dependence.

## 1. AgentDiscover: Autonomous Discovery with Minimal Search Scaffolding

**Authors/lab:** Mahdi Farahbakhsh, Ilan Sela, Fatemeh Doudi, Vishnu Teja Kunde, Krishna Narayanan, Jean-Francois Chamberland and Dileep Kalathil, Texas A&M University. **Date/version:** October 4, 2026, v1. **Primary text:** [arXiv:2610.05334](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05334v1). Relevant evidence: §3, §4, Appendix C and Appendix D.

**What was measured.** A coding agent chooses search strategy, experiments, and queries a graph database containing attempts, ideas and lineage. A server evaluates submissions separately and returns scores plus steering messages; a meta agent can supply lessons and reusable query tools. The paper describes 24 problems spanning systems optimization, mathematics, seven AtCoder contests, GPU kernels, single-cell denoising and a simulated-processor kernel builder. Table 1 presents several denoising datasets as separate rows, so table rows should not be mistaken for independent replicated runs.

Comparisons use GPT-6 Sol at $10 per task for GPU kernels and denoising, and Claude Opus 5 at $100 per task for the remaining Table 1 tasks. Five further AtCoder contests use Opus 5 and matched model spend of $120 to $200 per contest. Eleven systems/mathematical tasks use Opus 4.6 and at most 100 submitted candidates, matching published same-model baselines. The kernel-builder comparison runs all frameworks with Opus 5, low reasoning effort and a shared evaluator, at common model-spend checkpoints. Closed-model parameter counts are not disclosed in the paper. Each main-table entry is the best candidate in **one run**, not a mean across seeds.

**Actual findings.** On the eleven same-model optimization tasks, AgentDiscover leads seven and ties four at the reporting precision used. In kernel building, it reaches 1,016 cycles at $25 of model spend, beating the other frameworks' results even at $118; at $100 it reaches 960 cycles versus the strongest compared baseline's 1,098. The eleven-task comparison matches candidate counts rather than dollar spend, because the published baselines do not disclose spend. It therefore cannot independently establish a cost advantage on those eleven tasks.

Its own ablations qualify a simple “fewer agents is better” interpretation. Four parallel Haiku 4.5 search agents versus one, with five runs per setting on transaction scheduling, improve progress per elapsed runtime but show no clear advantage at equal model spend. Meta guidance, tested through 15 forks of five search runs, helps the next search agent improve within two candidates in 73% of forks versus 33% without guidance; final best fitness is higher in 10 forks, tied in two and lower in three. Search uses Haiku 4.5 and the meta agent uses Opus 4.6. The meta agent can help even within a system advertised as minimally scaffolded.

**Limits and meaning of controls.** The main results have single-run uncertainty and some baselines are published results rather than fresh matched reruns. Comparisons with TTT-Discover additionally change the model/training regime; those comparisons do not isolate harness effects. The paper replaces fixed search-selection heuristics while retaining a database, session resets, steering, a meta agent and isolated evaluation. It does not test removing the final evaluator.

Appendix C is directly relevant to Bigbrane. The authors report favorable scores for invalid output lengths, candidate-rebound scoring functions, candidate-controlled serialized objects, and leaked future workloads. In one signal-processing example, changing the scoring function produces a reported score of 200 million. Their corrections separate candidate code from scoring, validate outputs and withhold private data and candidate stdout from feedback. They also acknowledge that a corrected signal-processing metric still rewards oversmoothing above the exact ground truth. An independent scorer needs a scientifically appropriate objective as well as process separation.

## 2. Harness Annealing: Learning to Act with Less External Control

**Authors/lab:** Yingxuan Yang, Huacan Chai and Ying Wen, Shanghai Jiao Tong University and UC Berkeley. **Date/version:** October 1, 2026, v1. **Primary text:** [arXiv:2610.01235](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01235v1). Relevant evidence: §3, §4 and Tables 1 to 3.

**What was measured.** Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B are adapted using GPT-5.5 teacher trajectories. A bank of 5,007 filtered trajectories comes from 464 SWE-QA-Pro training questions. Evaluation covers **720 SWE-QA questions and 260 SWE-QA-Pro test questions**, each under four deployment harnesses: H1 tools only; H2 adds history, progress ledger and repetition detection; H3 adds a Locate/Read/Connect/Answer workflow; H4 adds heuristic draft checks and bounded repair. Every checkpoint is evaluated under every harness, giving 2,880 and 1,040 episodes per checkpoint respectively. The tasks are read-only repository question answering, without repository modification.

Training uses explicit control-decision supervision and a curriculum progressively withdrawing support, with replay and cue dropout. It changes model weights through LoRA. Main results use thinking-enabled evaluation with a 65,536-token context and a 32-step limit. The metric is a mean **GPT-5.5 reference-based rubric score out of 100**, formed from five scores of 1 to 20, rather than executable-test success.

**Actual findings.** The 9B H2-targeted checkpoint improves tools-only scores from 71.99 to 77.00 on SWE-QA and from 74.64 to 77.73 on SWE-QA-Pro relative to the full-harness-trained starting checkpoint deployed tools-only. The starting checkpoint with H4 scores 75.70 and 78.25. Thus, selected trained checkpoints can operate with less runtime support at comparable task scores. For 35B-A3B, corresponding tools-only improvements are smaller, from 80.60 to 81.00 and from 81.82 to 83.18. The unadapted larger model already has a narrower cross-harness score range: 2.48 and 2.14 points versus 7.35 and 8.44 for 9B. Further annealing does not consistently improve performance.

**Limits and meaning of controls.** This is evidence for **learned internalization of specified control decisions**, with additional training, rather than evidence that a new frontier model can automatically discard every harness layer. Two scales and two related QA benchmarks do not establish a scaling law. The final-answer judge does not inspect intermediate observations and therefore cannot directly verify evidential grounding. The removed “verification” layer is heuristic draft checking, without reference-answer access. Tools, execution guards and token budgets remain external at every level. Independent scientific scoring and hidden-data protection are outside the support-removal intervention.

## 3. How Much of a Harness Does a Strong Agent Need for Autonomous ML Engineering?

**Authors/lab:** Kirill Brilliantov, Alejandro Hernández-Cano and Emmanuel Abbé, EPFL and Apple. **Date/version:** September 30, 2026, v1. **Primary text:** [arXiv:2609.40303](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40303v1). Relevant evidence: §3 to §5 and Appendices A, D and G.

**What was measured.** A common OpenCode-based implementation compares chat completion plus external debugging, a tool-equipped coding session, Chain/Greedy/UCB1/Best-of-N search, and Malena, a single continuing coding-agent session. Additional interventions add delegated subagents, three parallel sessions and peer broadcast. Fresh comparisons cover MLEvolve, AiScientist, Arbor and ScienceFlow under matched backbone, candidate-execution hardware and runtime budgets.

The main external-harness cohort contains **30 of 75 MLE-bench tasks**, including five Lite, 21 Medium and four High tasks. Earlier intervention experiments use an original 29-task cohort; coordination ablations use a selected 14-task subset. NatureBench covers **40 tasks**, with three compared harnesses and GLM 5.2/Kimi K3. Candidate hardware is one A100 80GB, 12 CPU cores and 144GB RAM. Main MLE runs receive a 24-hour execution budget, NatureBench eight hours. The authors report three to four seeds per task for the 24-hour conditions and six to ten for shorter/completion conditions, with some uneven seed counts. These are measured experimental budgets, not project estimates.

Backbones include GLM 5.2, Kimi K3, Gemma 4 31B, gpt-oss-120b, Agents A1 and DeepSeek V4 Flash/Pro variants. The paper does not give a uniform parameter-count table for all backbones. Metrics distinguish **self-selection using agent validation** from **oracle selection using hidden test scores**, and report human-leaderboard percentile and any-medal rate with seed-bootstrap confidence intervals conditional on the task cohort.

**Actual findings.** Direct environment access produces the largest intervention effect. Among the manually imposed search strategies, no pair has a statistically supported advantage in percentile or medal rate; the largest percentile contrast, UCB1 over Best-of-N, is 2.71 points with a 95% interval from -1.44 to +6.60. Malena has a supported medal-rate advantage over those search strategies, although its percentile differences from UCB1 and Greedy remain indistinguishable. Delegation, parallelism and broadcast do not improve self-selected performance over baseline Malena in the tested coordination setting. Higher oracle ceilings from parallel search can be lost through poorer final-candidate selection.

The external-harness comparisons support the authors' claim that elaborate machinery provides little demonstrated advantage at the tested capable backbones. The Gemma 4 31B point estimates are an exception, favoring MLEvolve, with overlapping medal-rate intervals. On NatureBench with Kimi K3, AiScientist's point estimate is also slightly higher than Malena's, 27.1% versus 26.7%, with overlapping intervals. “Matches or beats” should therefore be read as statistical comparability or advantage within these experiments, rather than every Malena point estimate being highest.

**Limits and meaning of controls.** This is the best matched evidence here for reducing MLE search scaffolding, with finite power and task-selection limits. There is a reasoning-effort exception: MLEvolve with DeepSeek V4 Flash uses medium effort while others use their maximum settings. The authors repair several benchmark data/exposure defects for the external comparisons, so cohort versions need attention. MLE hidden scores are withheld from agents; NatureBench permits repeated online scoring, which differs from Bigbrane's protected confirmation lane.

Cost remains a harness issue. At GLM 5.2, Malena's **modeled inference cost is $12.12 versus AiScientist's $1.95**, attributed mainly to rereading its growing context. Cache use is modeled with a uniform discount rather than measured provider billing. The authors say candidate-execution hardware dominates total cost in their setup. Peak score comparability therefore does not establish inference-cost equivalence, and a long single session may be less suitable under tighter context or inference budgets.

## 4. What Happens When the Model Eats the Stack? Rethinking the Research Agenda for Data Agents to Withstand the Bitter Lesson

**Authors/lab:** Liana Patel, Siddharth Jha, Negar Arabzadeh, Carlos Guestrin, Ion Stoica and Matei Zaharia, Stanford and UC Berkeley. **Date/version:** September 2, 2026, v1. **Primary text:** [arXiv:2609.03141](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03141v1). Relevant evidence: §2 and Appendix A.

**What was measured.** o3, GPT-5 and GPT-5.6 Sol run in a common Codex coding-agent harness on TAG-Bench and Data-Agent Benchmark, with comparisons to an Agentar-inspired SQL pipeline and DeepEye's multi-agent analysis workflow. Closed-model sizes are unspecified. Appendix A identifies **60 non-aggregation TAG queries** for the Agentar-inspired comparison. Context generation uses **12 development queries and 12 datasets**, then evaluates on the remaining held-out DAB tasks; the paper text inspected does not clearly give the total DAB evaluation count or repeated-seed protocol. Metrics include answer accuracy, tokens, turns, latency and model-classified failure categories.

**Actual findings.** The specialized TAG pipeline helps o3, while general coding agents with GPT-5.6 Sol outperform the compared specialized pipelines. Within the general coding-agent setup, DAB accuracy improves by over 35 percentage points from o3 to GPT-5.6 Sol, token efficiency improves by over 2×, and turns per task fall by about 4×. The remaining failures are predominantly semantic/environmental, according to model-based trajectory classification. Offline self-curated accuracy context adds 19 accuracy points over the no-context agent on held-out DAB tasks. Schema-focused context reduces schema exploration but modestly lowers accuracy; added context can also raise latency.

**Limits and meaning of controls.** This is a research-agenda paper supported by comparative experiments, without a controlled size-only intervention. Its Agentar comparison is explicitly an approximation: the complete original pipeline is unavailable, so the baseline omits unreleased multi-generator, revisor and selector components. The trend cannot be generalized to every specialized data agent. Fewer verification/recovery turns are observed behavior under newer models; there is no experiment establishing safe removal of an independent verifier. The paper also argues for persistent external context, so it supplies evidence for simplifying prescribed workflows while preserving environment knowledge.

## 5. Beyond the Model: Demystifying Harness Effects in Software Engineering Agents

**Authors/lab:** Haichuan Hu, Quanjun Zhang, Shengcheng Yu, Zhifei Chen, Tianyu Luo, Chunrong Fang, Zhenyu Chen and Liang Xiao; Nanjing University of Science and Technology, Technical University of Munich and Nanjing University. **Date/version:** September 26, 2026, v1. **Primary text:** [arXiv:2609.32459](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32459v1). Relevant evidence: §4, §5 and §7.

**What was measured.** mini-SWE-agent versus OpenCode with ten models: Qwen3-Max, Qwen3.5-Plus, Qwen3.6-Plus, Qwen3.6-Max, Qwen3.7-Max; DeepSeek-R1, V3, V3.1, V3.2 and V4-Pro. Strength is ranked by Coding Index rather than parameter count. All run in non-thinking mode at temperature zero. Matched comparisons use **300 sampled SWE-bench Pro tasks, 70 sampled ProgramBench tasks and all 54 GitTaskBench tasks**. Component ablations use **all 200 ProgramBench tasks**, adding a tool registry, compression, explicit planning, generic or task-specific subagents, and lazy skills to form NanoHarness. Step limits are shared within tasks: 250 for issue/repository tasks and 300 for ProgramBench. Scores and tool/token behavior are measured; the inspected setup does not specify a repeated-seed protocol or confidence intervals for the main component effects.

**Actual findings.** On issue repair, stronger Qwen models reduce OpenCode's advantage from about six to 2.67 percentage points. DeepSeek's trend is non-monotonic, ending at 34.3% for OpenCode versus 33.7% for mini-SWE-agent with V4-Pro. On open-ended repository generation and repository tasks, stronger models become better able to exploit the complex harness.

On full ProgramBench, NanoHarness raises Qwen3.7-Max's score from 42.88 to 50.25 and V4-Pro's from 45.14 to 51.35. Task-specific subagents and structured tools yield the most consistent individual gains. Generic subagents hurt, while compression lowers scores by 4.86 and 3.85 points despite reducing prompt tokens by 54.29% and 77.02%. Planning and skills have smaller or model-dependent gains. The ProgramBench score includes partial test credit, as the paper's per-task examples show; these values should not be labeled whole-repository pass rates.

**Limits and meaning of controls.** This is direct counter-evidence to a task-independent claim of declining harness value. It covers two model families and five component classes, with task-dependent effects. Non-thinking evaluation and finite step limits constrain applicability to frontier thinking-mode deployments. Its introductory leaderboard comparisons are observational; the controlled experiments provide better evidence. Permission, environment, logging and evaluation boundaries are outside the component mechanisms analyzed, and containerized evaluations retain isolation restrictions.

## 6. VERSE: Verified Self-Evolving Optimizer for Agent Harnesses

**Authors/lab:** Zekai Wang, Yingqiang Ge, Zekun Wang, Hai Wang, Yuhui Xu, Joshua Frandsen, Shancong Fu, Ashia C. Wilson and Chandan K. Reddy, MIT and Amazon. **Date/version:** October 2, 2026, v1. **Primary text:** [arXiv:2610.02616](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02616v1). Relevant evidence: §2, §4 and Tables 1 to 3.

**What was measured.** A frozen Qwen3.8-Flash-Next optimizer, **176B total/6B active**, edits harnesses for a frozen Qwen3.8-27B executor. Six rounds produce three candidate executor harnesses per round. Interventions let the optimizer execute draft changes, replay failures, perturb suspected causes, keep training audits, and revise its own prompts/tools/skills/hooks/notes. Four baseline optimizer approaches are reimplemented under the protocol.

SWE-rebench splits contain **110 training, 50 validation, 108 in-distribution test and 107 later out-of-distribution test tasks**. The latter span five languages, with 87 non-Python tasks. Accuracy is the fraction resolved, averaged over three evaluations with standard error. There is **one harness-evolution run per reported row**; three final evaluations do not replicate the optimization process itself. Validation selects the deployment harness, and test tasks are sealed from the optimizer.

**Actual findings.** In the motivating study, self-evolution without draft execution checks fails to beat the initial harness on validation. With checks, the self-evolving optimizer reaches 41.05% test accuracy. The main Meta-Harness-plus-VERSE result is 42.28% in distribution and 37.69% out of distribution, versus the unequipped Meta-Harness's 39.20% and 28.66%. Across hosts, VERSE with self-evolution beats each host's unequipped baseline in the reported comparisons, although self-evolution does not beat VERSE without self-evolution in every cell.

Execution logs show how fallible harness-edit proposals remain: only **17 of 170 draft evaluations** fix a targeted failing task. Submitted edits fix 148 and break 134 validation outcomes that reproduce on rerun, aggregated over 18 evolution runs. Half of initial validation outcome changes fail to reproduce. This supports testing candidate improvements and regressions rather than accepting the optimizer's interpretation of a trajectory alone.

**Limits and meaning of controls.** This studies optimization of harnesses for a 27B executor, without a size-scaling ablation. Total evolution compute differs across methods; equal proposal limits do not make it an equal-compute comparison. Verification is execution feedback during harness development. It is distinct from Bigbrane's scientific confirmation protocol, although the sealed-test design is relevant. Verification gains vary by host: one AHE ablation without self-evolution changes validation-selected test accuracy only from 37.96% to 37.65% when verification tools are removed. The factorial motivating result should not be generalized into a claim that every verification tool always pays for itself.

## 7. HarnessSQL: Harness-Native Training for SQL Agents in Realistic Database Environments

**Authors/lab:** Haolin Yang, Jipeng Zhang, Zhiqiang Qian, Jian Xie, Shuaishuai Gong, Yike Guo and Sirui Han; affiliations include HKUST, Microsoft Research, Tsinghua University and University of Macau. **Date/version:** October 8, 2026, v1. **Primary text:** [arXiv:2610.12274](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12274v1). Relevant evidence: §4, §5, §7 and Appendix G.

**What was measured.** Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B receive full-interaction supervised fine-tuning followed by execution-reward RL in a four-tool SQL harness. Training uses **79 databases, 2,512 verified teacher trajectories, 2,700 synthesized RL prompts and 100 validation instances**. Main evaluation includes **135 Spider 2.0-SQLite tasks**, BIRD-Interact Mini with a GPT-4o user simulator, and LiveSQLBench Base-Lite SQLite. Counts for the latter two are not clearly stated in the inspected benchmark appendix. Metrics are execution accuracy and interaction/protocol behavior. Environments retain read-only database bindings, isolated task execution, deadlines, bounded observations and hidden oracles.

**Actual findings.** With the interaction harness retained, training improves 8B Spider execution accuracy from 15.5% to 45.2%, and 14B from 22.2% to 54.8%. An 8B ablation isolates several effects: no-training/no-harness scores 2.2%; adding the SQL harness gives 15.5%; conventional non-harness training deployed through that harness scores 9.6%; harness-native training deployed through it scores 30.4%. The main 45.2% result additionally includes RL. This supports environment access and training/deployment alignment, with compact models outperforming some larger static-query baselines.

**Limits and meaning of controls.** The main before/after result changes weights and training objective while preserving a harness. It cannot establish that harness complexity alone causes all the gains, or that larger frontier models need elaborate scaffolding. SQLite is the primary scope. A reporting inconsistency warrants caution: §4 says comparison models use native inference protocols, while Appendix G says all models are uniformly integrated via DSH-SQL. The within-model ablation is more interpretable than a headline comparison across dissimilar models and protocols. Hidden execution verification remains part of the method throughout.

## 8. JIT-Agent: Scaling Harness Intelligence via Just-in-Time Harness Evolution

**Authors/lab:** Guibin Zhang and 17 coauthors, listed as the JIT-Agent Team in the HTML text. **Dates/version:** first submitted August 26, 2026; v2 September 3, 2026. **Primary sources:** [submission history and authors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25593), [v2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25593v2). Relevant evidence: §5 and Tables 2 to 3.

**What was measured.** A trained Qwen3.6-27B helper generates task-specific memory, planning, action and capability-orchestration modules around frozen execution models. Main backbones are GLM 5.2 and DeepSeek V4 Flash Preview. Nine benchmarks cover deep research, daily work, planning and workspace execution, using accuracy, F1, rubric, constraint and success metrics on a 0 to 100 scale. Controlled comparisons also run DeepSeek V4 Flash and Qwen3.6-Flash through Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Hermes, NanoBot and JIT-generated harnesses, measuring score, tokens and API cost. A six-backbone generalization study uses **100 DeepSearchQA examples and 50 examples each for AgentIF, shopping planning and OfficeBench**. The main nine-benchmark setup does not clearly disclose all evaluation counts or repeated-test uncertainty. Executor parameter counts are not uniformly specified.

**Actual findings.** Replacing default scaffolds improves all 18 matched main backbone/benchmark pairs. Mean normalized score rises from 74.1 to 81.8 for GLM 5.2 and 66.7 to 75.5 for DeepSeek V4 Flash. Across six controlled production-harness comparisons, JIT has the highest score in four and the lowest reported API cost in all six. For example, DeepSeek on DeepSearchQA scores 85.1 at $0.066 per case versus NanoBot's 80.4 at $0.131. The 24 matched generalization comparisons against ReAct all favor generated harnesses, with a reported average gain of 7.6 points.

**Limits and meaning of controls.** This is counter-evidence that task-adaptive scaffolding can still improve capable backbones. An additional trained model generates the scaffolding, so the intervention includes learned support beyond the executor. Averaging different normalized task metrics supplies a summary index rather than a common accuracy measure. Incomplete count/uncertainty disclosure limits assessment of the precision and reproducibility of headline margins. Results do not validate removing a trusted evaluator, nor establish scientific-discovery performance for Bigbrane.

## What this means for Bigbrane

### Capability scaffolding we can keep thin

The papers justify a **single coding-agent research loop as a required comparison**, with the same model, tools, development data and budget as the pinned RD-Agent integration. The strongest applicable evidence comes from the matched MLE study and AgentDiscover's computational search tasks. Let the model choose which hypothesis or candidate to investigate next before adding fixed tree search, obligatory planner/reviewer roles, generic delegation or extra retry layers. Add a capability component when a matched experiment shows better valid discoveries or lower total cost. Beyond the Model and JIT-Agent show why components need task-specific measurement, rather than universal removal.

Retain a queryable attempt history and environment context. AgentDiscover's graph stores losers and lineage; the data-agent study finds benefits from persistent context. Those results fit Bigbrane's evaluator/ledger/schemas plus RD-Agent core hybrid. They do not require adopting another complete platform. The pinned RD-Agent interfaces can represent experiments, feedback and ancestry while imposing less search policy. Harness Annealing suggests that trained models can internalize some process decisions, but Bigbrane cannot assume those gains without comparable adaptation or direct evidence from its deployed model.

Compare **valid falsifiable experiments, development performance, failures retained, total model/execution spend and final-candidate selection quality**. Keep multiple independent search runs where feasible and avoid ranking systems by one best trajectory. Use identical feedback authority in matched arms. MLE's oracle/self-selection distinction shows why the best candidate generated and the candidate correctly selected for confirmation are separate outcomes. Inference cost and candidate-execution cost should both appear in the comparison.

### Verification and trust boundaries remain outside the agent's authority

Keep the exact-oracle computation and final scoring outside candidate code's process and writable workspace. Validate candidate outputs against the declared mathematical and data constraints. Restrict artifacts to the expected data formats; candidate-controlled serialization must not execute inside the scorer. Keep hidden data, confirmation seeds and evaluator credentials inaccessible to the agent and candidate execution. Record hypotheses, equations, data design, falsifiers, attempt IDs, hashes and failures in the application-owned ledger. Freeze candidates before fresh confirmation. These are recommendations for Bigbrane, supported most directly by AgentDiscover's documented scoring and leakage failures; the capability ablations provide no evidence for weakening them.

The scorer's objective also needs validation. AgentDiscover's oversmoothing example shows that even a process-separated evaluator can reward an invalid scientific proxy. For Bigbrane, test that reported scores correspond to the declared causal prediction protocol, oracle target and falsifier. Model-written analysis may guide development; it cannot substitute for the independent numeric result or confer confirmation status on itself. VERSE's low draft-fix rate further argues for executed evidence before adopting model-proposed harness changes.

The existing [platform trial](PLATFORM-TRIAL.md) exercised trusted fixtures, scripted proposals and **zero live model calls**. It does not establish discovery quality, model/harness superiority or generated-code isolation. The [platform selection](PLATFORM-SELECTION.md) already calls for a separately controlled evaluator and protected confirmation lane. The literature supports testing a thinner research controller inside that plan. It supplies no grounds to remove those boundaries or to replace the selected platform without a matched Bigbrane capability trial.

## Reading order and unresolved evidence

Read AgentDiscover first for its discovery loop and evaluator failures, then the matched MLE study for controlled scaffold comparisons, followed by Harness Annealing for the precise meaning of “less external control.” Beyond the Model and VERSE provide the most directly relevant qualifications. The data-agent paper, JIT-Agent and HarnessSQL extend the picture to persistent context, task-adaptive scaffolds and training alignment.

The unresolved question is whether Bigbrane's deployed model can generate and select better mathematical-learning experiments with a thin loop than with RD-Agent's prescribed policies. None of these papers measures Bigbrane, its exact-oracle target or its fresh-confirmation protocol. The evidence favors running that comparison while keeping verification authority fixed.
